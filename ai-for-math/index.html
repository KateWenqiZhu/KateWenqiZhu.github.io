Traceable multi-agent mathematics · Oxford

One lead, many lanes, every step on the record

A human expert chooses the question. A lead agent splits it into lanes, each run by a frontier model from one of several companies. Every claim is reviewed by a model from a different company, machine-checked where possible, and kept with its full evidence. This page lists what has come out of it, ranked by importance, with an honest status on each result.

Cross-company by design

No single lab's model checks its own work. Lanes run on models from different companies (Anthropic's Claude, OpenAI's Codex, DeepSeek and Qwen). A result is reviewed by a model from another company, and the Lean kernel is the neutral referee.

Teachable and traceable

Every lemma, number and design choice points back to a source, a computation or a named lane, so a specialist can follow it.

Every theorem becomes a skill

A finished proof is distilled into a reusable, checkable procedure, so the next problem starts further ahead.

Not scattered knowledge

Results are joined into one atlas. Next comes SoS Atlas: which classes of polynomials have small certificates, and how to find them.

How a result earns its place

1

An exact question

The human expert fixes the statement first. A weaker statement is never substituted to make a proof pass.

2

Lanes in parallel

The lead agent opens lanes with briefs, kill criteria and budgets. Failed routes are kept, with the exact obstruction.

3

Checked evidence

Exact arithmetic, fail-closed checkers with negative controls, and Lean proofs that print only the standard axioms.

4

Cross-vendor review

The model that produced a proof never reviews it. Only reviewed statements enter the record; humans write every manuscript.

Papers and manuscripts

AI produces experiment logs. We understand, trace and check every step, and we write the papers ourselves. * marks the lead author. Full list on the publications page.

    About the Atlas

    The Atlas was started by researchers at the Mathematical Institute, University of Oxford, and is led by Kate Wenqi Zhu. It began in autumn 2026 as a test of a simple idea: AI systems from several companies can work on hard mathematics together, provided every step is recorded, checked by a model from a different company, and understood by a human before anything is claimed.

    Ping Nie is the main collaborator on the catalogue results. Co-authors of each result are named on its card. The AI lanes run on models from Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepSeek and Qwen; the people are responsible for every statement and every manuscript.
